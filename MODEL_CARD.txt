🧠 Model Card — Multimodal AI for Head and Neck Lymphoid Lesion Classification

1. Model Overview
This repository provides a multimodal artificial intelligence framework developed to support the classification of head and neck lymphoid lesions using histopathological image analysis, nuclear morphometric descriptors, and clinicopathological variables.

The model was designed for four diagnostic categories:
| Class | Diagnostic Category |
|-------|--------------------------------|
|   0   |   Aggressive B-cell lymphoma   |
|   1   | Indolent/small B-cell lymphoma |
|   2   |       NK/T-cell lymphoma       |
|   3   |     Reactive lymphoid lesion   |

The computational framework integrates:
- UNI foundation model embeddings extracted from H&E histopathological patches
- CellViT++-based nuclear segmentation and morphometric feature extraction
- Clinicopathological variables, including age, sex, and anatomical location
- Attention-based multiple instance learning (MIL)
- Patch-level, slide-level, and patient-level evaluation
- SHAP-based explainability analysis
- AI-guided diagnostic workflow for immunohistochemical and molecular testing
The goal of this model is to provide a research-oriented decision-support framework for computational hematopathology, particularly in diagnostically challenging head and neck lymphoid lesions.

This model is not intended for autonomous clinical diagnosis.

2. Study Design and Data
This study was designed as a multicentre, retrospective, cross-sectional investigation using anonymised histopathological and clinicopathological data.
Cases were retrieved from pathology archives between 2000 and 2024 and included head and neck lymphoid lesions reviewed by specialist pathologists.

The dataset included the following diagnostic groups:

| Diagnostic Group | Number of Cases |
|---------------------------------|-----|
|   Aggressive B-cell lymphomas   | 278 |
| Indolent/small B-cell lymphomas | 128 |
|        NK/T-cell lymphomas      |  86 |
|    Reactive lymphoid lesions    | 170 |

The cases were obtained from multiple institutions across Brazil, Guatemala, Mexico, and the United Kingdom. The multicentre design introduced variability in staining protocols, scanner platforms, tissue processing, and institutional workflows, allowing evaluation of model robustness under heterogeneous real-world conditions.
Whole-slide images were scanned at 40× magnification using digital pathology systems, and representative regions of interest were annotated for downstream analysis.

3. Data Splitting
The dataset was divided into development and independent external validation cohorts.
The development cohort included 80% of the dataset and was subdivided into:
- Training set: 60%
- Validation set: 10%
- Internal test set: 10%

The remaining 20% of cases were reserved for independent external validation and divided into:
- External Validation Cohort A
- External Validation Cohort B
Data splitting was performed in a centre-aware manner to reduce institutional data leakage and to support robust multicentre evaluation.

4. Model Architecture
4.1 UNI Foundation Model
The UNI foundation model was used as a frozen feature extractor for histopathological image representation learning.

Main characteristics:
- Backbone: Vision Transformer `vit_large_patch16_224`
- Input: H&E image patches
- Patch size: 224 × 224 pixels
- Encoder: Frozen UNI model
- Output: High-dimensional patch embeddings
- Training: No UNI weight updates

The extracted embeddings were used for:
- Patch-level classification
- MIL-based slide-level aggregation
- Patient-level prediction
- Multimodal fusion with morphometric and clinical data

4.2 Patch-Level Classifier

UNI-derived embeddings were passed to a fully connected classifier:
text
Linear(d → 256)
ReLU
Dropout(0.3)
Linear(256 → 4)

4.3 Multiple Instance Learning Model

An attention-based MIL module was used to aggregate patch embeddings into slide-level representations.

Attention mechanism:
Linear(d → 128)
Tanh
Linear(128 → 1)
Softmax

Slide-level classifier:
Linear(d → 128)
ReLU
Dropout(0.2)
Linear(128 → 4)

This allowed the model to assign attention weights to diagnostically informative patches and generate slide-level predictions.

4.4 CellViT++ Nuclear Segmentation

CellViT++ was used for nuclear instance segmentation and morphometric feature extraction.
The original CellViT++ architecture was used without architectural modifications.

Extracted nuclear morphometric features included:
Nuclear area
Nuclear perimeter
Circularity
Eccentricity
These features were computed from segmentation masks and aggregated at patch and case levels.

4.5 Multimodal Fusion

The multimodal model integrated:
UNI-derived histopathological embeddings
Nuclear morphometric descriptors
Clinicopathological variables

Clinical variables included:
Age
Sex
Anatomical location

Morphometric variables included:
Nuclear area
Perimeter
Circularity
Eccentricity

All structured features were standardised and concatenated with image-derived embeddings before supervised classification.

Training characteristics:

8. Explainability

SHAP was used to quantify the contribution of each input variable to model predictions.

The most influential features were:
Nuclear area
Nuclear perimeter
Nuclear eccentricity
Circularity

Clinical variables such as age, sex, and anatomical location showed comparatively lower SHAP magnitudes.
The prominence of nuclear area and related morphometric features is biologically consistent with established pathological criteria for high-grade lymphomas, where increased nuclear size, pleomorphism, and chromatin irregularity are diagnostically relevant.
This supports the interpretability and biological plausibility of the multimodal model.

9. AI-Guided Diagnostic Workflow

The model outputs were incorporated into a structured diagnostic support workflow.

The model generates probabilistic predictions across four diagnostic categories:
Aggressive B-cell lymphoma
Indolent/small B-cell lymphoma
NK/T-cell lymphoma
Reactive lesion

High-confidence predictions guide targeted first-line immunohistochemical panels.
Intermediate or uncertain predictions trigger reflex immunohistochemical testing.

Persistently ambiguous cases are escalated to molecular testing, including:
FISH for MYC, BCL2, BCL6, and CCND1 rearrangements
PCR-based clonality assays for IGH/TCR
EBV detection using EBER in situ hybridisation
The system is intended to support pathologist decision-making and optimise diagnostic workflows, not to replace expert interpretation.

11. Limitations

Key limitations include:
Retrospective study design
Multicentre variability in staining, scanning, and tissue processing
Potential residual institutional bias
Limited availability of external validation cohorts
No public release of whole-slide images due to ethical restrictions
Dependence on quality of ROI selection and tissue preservation
Model outputs require expert pathological interpretation

Although the model demonstrated good external performance, further validation across larger and more diverse international cohorts is required.

12. Potential Biases

Potential sources of bias include:
Institutional differences in tissue processing
Scanner-specific image characteristics
Variation in staining intensity
Uneven diagnostic category distribution
Geographic differences in lymphoma prevalence
Selection bias from available archival material
ROI-based sampling bias

Centre-aware splitting was used to reduce data leakage and support robust external validation.

13. Ethical and Responsible AI Considerations

All data were anonymised and used under institutional ethical approval and data transfer agreements.
Due to patient privacy regulations and institutional policies:
Whole-slide images are not publicly distributed
Raw clinicopathological data are not publicly shared
Patient-identifiable metadata are not included in this repository
The model is intended as a research and decision-support tool only.
Predictions must always be interpreted by qualified pathologists in conjunction with histology, immunohistochemistry, molecular findings, and clinical context.
